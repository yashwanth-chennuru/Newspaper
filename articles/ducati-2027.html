Ducati is tipped for an early edge in MotoGP’s 2027 rules reset

Early 850cc testing and existing Pirelli knowledge have made Ducati the early reference point, according to KTM tester Pol Espargaró.

MotoGP will change substantially in 2027. The championship moves from 1,000cc to 850cc bikes, swaps Michelin for Pirelli tyres, further restricts aerodynamics and bans ride-height devices.

The next-generation prototypes are already accumulating test mileage. After attending a Mugello test that included all manufacturers except Yamaha, KTM tester Pol Espargaró said Ducati and test rider Nicolò Bulega had been convincingly quick.

Espargaró’s view is not that the order is settled. He expects the field to converge over time, perhaps by the second year of the rule cycle, but thinks some manufacturers may begin faster than others.

He singled out Ducati’s World Superbike success with the Panigale V4 R, which already runs on Pirelli tyres, as a logical reason it could understand the new tyre behaviour sooner than rivals.

It is still too early to call a 2027 hierarchy: testing conditions and development programmes are opaque. But Ducati’s current form and relevant Pirelli experience make it an obvious team to watch through the technical transition.
