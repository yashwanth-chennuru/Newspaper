Cadillac’s development is not yet solving its underlying problems

Incremental aero revisions have been overshadowed by persistent front-brake cooling failures, argues Gary Anderson.

Gary Anderson’s analysis of Cadillac’s 2026 development finds a team still seeking a meaningful step forward. As a new entrant, Cadillac began with a large deficit to close, but its recent updates have not yet pointed to a clear aerodynamic breakthrough.

Across the front wing, endplate and footplate areas, Anderson sees changes that look evolutionary: small geometry adjustments and circuit-specific trimming rather than a new airflow concept. Those updates may still be useful, but he does not see them transforming the car’s direction.

The urgent weakness is front-brake cooling. Cadillac has repeatedly had cars retire after front brakes caught fire, including another incident after it enlarged the inlet at Hungary.

Anderson’s point is that a larger inlet alone is not the answer. Cooling needs a pressure drop through the duct: the outlet must be well matched and placed in a low-pressure area, otherwise the incoming flow cannot do enough work.

The story is a useful technical read on why reliability and packaging can cap a new team as much as peak aerodynamic load. Cadillac’s development appears to be addressing details, but its brake issue remains a system-level problem to solve.
