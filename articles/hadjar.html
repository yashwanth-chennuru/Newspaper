Hadjar says race-to-race consistency is the gap to Verstappen

The Red Bull driver believes raw pace is often close; repeating it through every weekend is the harder next step.

Isack Hadjar has made a strong early impression after his promotion to Red Bull. Motorsport.com reports that the 21-year-old sees his 2026 level as a normal improvement in his second season rather than a surprise to himself.

His own comparison with Max Verstappen is revealing. Hadjar believes he is close on pace at certain events, but says he needs to learn how to reproduce that level at every circuit and in every set of conditions.

That is a more demanding target than one-lap speed. It includes reading a changing weekend, extracting a difficult car balance and making fewer costly compromises when the setup is not naturally in a driver’s window.

The context is Red Bull’s notoriously difficult second seat. Several experienced and talented drivers have struggled to maintain performance alongside Verstappen, so sustained consistency is the clearest proof point for anyone in that role.

Hadjar’s assessment is measured rather than dramatic: he identifies experience as the missing variable and frames improvement as a process. The second half of the season will show whether that baseline can become repeatable.
